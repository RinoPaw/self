# Daniel Skibra — Self-ascription and primitiveness (2026)

## Bibliography

Daniel Skibra, “Self-ascription and primitiveness”, *Review of Philosophy and Psychology*, published 24 March 2026.

DOI: `10.1007/s13164-026-00810-6`

## Why this matters here

This paper is unusually useful for the `Centered Total State` line because it separates two questions that are easy to conflate:

1. whether self-ascription / centering must be primitive;
2. whether the choice of center is arbitrary.

Skibra argues that the simple primitiveness complaint against Lewis is too crude. Functionalist and context-initialization reconstructions can explain substantial parts of self-ascription. But the arbitrariness objection remains harder to remove completely.

That is almost exactly the distinction needed in `self`:

\[
\text{center representation}
\neq
\text{center explanation}.
\]

---

## 1. Centered worlds

Lewis-style centered possibilities can be represented by:

\[
\langle w,\langle x,t\rangle\rangle.
\]

The center marks the standpoint of the attitude holder inside a world, analogous to a “you are here” marker on a map.

This gives enough representational granularity to distinguish ordinary de se states that uncentered possible worlds cannot distinguish.

But merely adding the coordinate does not yet explain why a given coordinate is the relevant self-center.

---

## 2. Evasion objection

A classic worry is that saying “the subject self-ascribes property F” merely gives a technical name to the first-person relation that needed explanation.

Skibra argues that Jackson–Stoljar’s functionalist reading weakens this objection.

On that reading, attitudes are individuated partly by causal / functional role; self-ascription labels a relation to fine-grained content that helps explain different patterns of action and reasoning.

So ordinary self-ascription need not be treated as a mysterious relation between a thinker and a specially privileged object.

### Relevance to this project

This shows:

\[
\boxed{\text{some forms of centering can be independently functionally motivated}}
\]

That is an important positive precedent against the claim that every center is automatically primitive.

---

## 3. Arbitrariness objection

A different objection survives:

once the theory has enough fine-grained centered contents, why is the privileged coordinate the subject herself rather than some other object / coordinate?

Skibra reconstructs Cappelen–Dever as pressing this problem: the content theory alone does not force one interpretation of the center rather than another.

In project language:

\[
\langle W,c\rangle
\]

is not explanatory merely because `c` appears in the tuple.

One still needs:

\[
M(W,\text{context})\Rightarrow c.
\]

---

## 4. Initialization strategy

Openshaw’s proposal uses Lewis’s context-index semantics.

A context supplies coordinates such as:

\[
\langle speaker,time,place,\ldots\rangle.
\]

Semantic evaluation initializes index parameters using the actual context. Self-ascription can then be understood as the relation to content evaluated using those context values.

This gives a principled reason for ordinary centering:

\[
\boxed{\text{the tokening context itself supplies the center coordinates}}
\]

rather than choosing Zeus / Odin coordinates arbitrarily.

Skibra regards this as a serious response, while arguing that arbitrariness is not fully dispatched in every case.

---

## 5. Why this does not solve absolute centering

The context strategy scales in the wrong direction for this project.

Each real attitude / experiential token has its own context:

\[
k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_n.
\]

So the same explanation gives:

\[
center(k_1),center(k_2),\ldots,center(k_n).
\]

That is exactly what ordinary first-person pluralism requires.

It does not yield:

\[
\exists!center_{absolute}.
\]

To get an absolute center using the same architecture, one needs a unique global context:

\[
K_G.
\]

No such context is supplied by the Lewis / Openshaw / Jackson–Stoljar framework.

Thus the literature provides a useful split:

\[
\boxed{\text{ordinary centering may be non-primitive}}
\]

while:

\[
\boxed{\text{absolute centering remains unexplained}}
\]

---

## 6. Research consequence

Skibra supplies a direct stress test for `Centered Total State`.

A successful centered-total-reality theory must answer both:

### Non-evasion

What independent relation / process makes the complete state centered?

### Non-arbitrariness

Why does that mechanism center the state on this experiential locus rather than another?

If the only answer is:

> because this is the center-coordinate of the actual centered state,

then the theory has likely only renamed the primitive.

If the answer appeals to a real global analogue of context / functional role, then that structure becomes the next object of study.
