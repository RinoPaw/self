## 2. Test A — Phenomenal residue

### 2.1 Strong positive intuition

The strongest positive tradition says that current experience is not merely temporally located differently from past/future experiences; it is phenomenally or existentially **live**, **available**, or **present** in a special way.

Relevant sources include:

- Yuri Balashov, `Times of Our Lives: Negotiating the Presence of Experience` (2005);
- L. A. Paul, `Temporal Experience` (2010);
- Barry Dainton, `Time, Passage, and Immediate Experience` (2011);
- Caspar Hare's tense/perspective work;
- the `presence of experience` argument discussed by Bradford Skow and Vincent Conitzer.

This positive pressure is real. It should not be caricatured as merely saying that we utter the word `now`.

### 2.2 Hoerl: no robust passage phenomenology need be present

Christoph Hoerl (2014), `Do We (Seem to) Perceive Passage?`, argues that the alleged phenomenology of passage need not exist in the first place. The phenomenology of perceived movement/change contains structural features that can lead us to judge that passage is experienced, without a further robust passage-quality being present.

This blocks a simple appeal to introspective obviousness:

\[
\boxed{
ExperienceOfChange
\not\Rightarrow
PhenomenologyOfRobustPassage.
}
\]

### 2.3 Deng/Fazekas: substantial temporal phenomenology can be B-compatible

Natalja Deng develops B-theoretic veridicalist accounts on which succession, temporal passivity, and temporal direction can ground important aspects of temporal experience without objective A-properties.

Katherine Fazekas (2019) decomposes passage experience into advancing-to-later-times, change, lack of control, and dynamic/flow-like phenomenology, then argues that the experience can veridically represent the temporal direction of causal processes and remain compatible with either A- or B-theory.

So even granting rich temporal phenomenology:

\[
\boxed{
RichTemporalPhenomenology
\not\Rightarrow
MonadicPresentness.
}
\]

### 2.4 Torrengo: perceptual presentness need not have a distinctive phenomenology

Giuliano Torrengo's 2024 work on presentness explicitly separates the perceptual and conceptual dimensions of the present and denies that perceptual presentness has a distinctive phenomenal character. Its prominence is explained through temporal location and narrative cognition.

That provides a recent explicit model of:

\[
PhenomenalNow
\to
TemporalViewpoint/NarrativeStructure
\]

without:

\[
PhenomenalNow
\to
PresentSimpliciter.
\]

### 2.5 2025–2026 empirical pressure

Kristie Miller (2025), `A Challenge for Experiential Passage Realism`, reports data according to which roughly 35% of participants do **not** report that it seems as though time robustly passes, while roughly 65% do.

This is important for a putative universal phenomenal witness. If robust passage/presence were a highly direct, theory-independent phenomenal datum, substantial stable non-report should at least be surprising and require explanation.

The pressure strengthened in Bigg, Latham, Miller & Yechimovitz (2026), `Agentive Explanations of Temporal Passage Experiences and Beliefs`: variation in agentive experience received empirical support as an explanation of variation in reports of robust temporal passage, while variation in reported passage-seeming did not explain variation in robust-passage belief.

This does not prove B-theory. It does, however, undermine the use of robust temporal seeming as a uniform, primitive, introspectively mandatory datum.

### 2.6 Verdict on Test A

No phenomenal residue has survived the requirement:

\[
Independent(P)
\land
RelationalModel\not\models P.
\]

The common phenomenological package can still be written as:

\[
P=\{Immediacy,PhenomenalNow,TemporalIntegration,Change,Agency,ForMeNess,Memory,Anticipation\}
\]

and interpreted relationally.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{TestA=FAIL.}
\]

## 3. Test B — Semantic indispensability

### 3.1 Prior's challenge is genuine

Arthur Prior's `Thank Goodness That's Over` argument shows that tensed attitudes cannot be naively replaced by dated/tenseless descriptions without losing psychological and semantic role.

Likewise, first-person and temporal indexicals have irreducible roles in thought and action.

But the project must keep the familiar distinction:

\[
IrreducibleRepresentation
\neq
IrreducibleWorldlyFact.
\]

### 3.2 New B-theory: ineliminable tense does not entail tensed facts

The New B-theory tradition explicitly concedes that tensed sentences may be untranslatable into tenseless sentences while denying that their truthmakers must include irreducibly tensed worldly facts.

Heather Dyke's surveys and arguments make the methodological point explicit:

\[
\boxed{
Semantic/LinguisticIrreducibility
\not\Rightarrow
MetaphysicalTense.
}
\]

Tenseless relations can supply truth conditions or truthmakers for irreducibly tensed discourse.

Dean Zimmerman (2005) likewise sharply distinguishes `taking tense seriously` in propositional attitudes from A-theoretic metaphysics. An eternalist who takes tense seriously has not thereby accepted a metaphysically privileged present.

### 3.3 Prosser: a person-reflexive answer to Prior

Simon Prosser (2016) develops a person-reflexive theory of temporal attitudes. The distinctive psychological role of a tensed attitude can be explained via a relation between a person-stage and a time/event.

This matters directly for the current project because the proposed centered witness is itself person-stage shaped.

Prosser shows that:

\[
PsychologicalRoleOfNow
\]

can be captured through:

\[
Relation(PersonStage,Time/Event)
\]

without an independently privileged `NOW simpliciter`.

### 3.4 Verdict on Test B

Semantic indispensability establishes at most:

\[
IrreducibleTemporalPerspectiveInThought.
\]

It does not establish:

\[
IrreducibleMonadicPresentnessInReality.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{TestB=FAIL.}
\]

This is structurally parallel to the project's Representation–Fact Gap on the first-person axis.

## 4. Test C — Explanatory residue

### 4.1 The strongest challenge: why are only these experiences available?

Balashov's challenge is stronger than a bare phenomenology argument. On the B-theory, all of a subject's experiences tenselessly occur. Yet only some seem currently available/presented.

The intended pressure is:

\[
EqualOntologicalStatus
+DifferentialAvailability
\Rightarrow
NeedPresentness.
\]

### 4.2 Skow's three responses

Bradford Skow (2011; expanded 2015) directly reconstructs this argument and gives three B-theoretic responses.

1. **Time-relative availability**: an experience is available to a subject at a time. Differential availability is grounded in relations to the temporal perspective from which availability is assessed.
2. **Stage view**: experiences can be available simpliciter to person-stages; distinct stages have distinct experiences.
3. **Perdurantist variant**: experiences may be available simpliciter to temporal parts/stages rather than to the persisting whole person.

The important project-level result is not that all these replies are costless. They are not. In particular, Balashov argues that the stage view is the best B-theoretic response.

The important result is:

\[
\boxed{
DifferentialAvailability
\not\Rightarrow
PresentnessSimpliciter.
}
\]

The neutral side has coherent explanatory architectures.

### 4.3 Cost is not residual impossibility

If the B-theorist must adopt a stage theory, that is a genuine theoretical cost. But CC1 requires more than a competitor cost. It requires a datum the competitor **cannot** explain without importing the target metaphysics.

Thus:

\[
RelationalExplanationRequiresStageTheory
\]

would not imply:

\[
RelationalExplanationFails.
\]

### 4.4 Recent explanatory work strengthens the neutral side

The 2025–2026 empirical work is also relevant here. If variation in robust-passage reports tracks agency-related variation, that supplies a concrete explanatory dimension unavailable to a simple `one universal robust-presentness phenomenology` picture.

Miller's 2026 *Life in Four Dimensions* develops a sustained deflationist IBE: reported temporal seemings are explained without robust passage, and non-deflationist explanations face difficulty accounting for variation.

Again, this is not a deductive refutation of A-theory. It shows that the neutral side is not explanatorily empty.

### 4.5 Verdict on Test C

No mandatory explanatory residue remains:

\[
\boxed{TestC=FAIL.}
\]

The strongest positive result is only a cost comparison among persistence/experience models.

## 5. Test D — Relational incoherence or regress

### 5.1 Target

A decisive route would prove:

\[
RelationalPresence
\vdash
\bot
\]

or generate a vicious regress that monadic presentness uniquely terminates.

### 5.2 No such contradiction was located

B-theoretic models based on earlier/later/simultaneous relations, temporal location, causal structure, memory, anticipation, and person-stage relations are mature and internally articulable.

They face substantive philosophical disputes about persistence, dynamicity, temporal asymmetry, and adequacy to experience. But the audit located no argument establishing that relational presence **as such** is incoherent.

### 5.3 Regress pressure is not asymmetrically favorable to A-theory

McTaggart's paradox famously generates regress/incompatibility pressure for A-properties themselves: an event must be future, present, and past in different tensed ways, and attempts to explain the compatibility can generate further tensed levels.

Modern A-theorists dispute McTaggart's conclusion, so this is not a refutation of A-theory.

But it blocks a cheap asymmetry in which `simpliciter presentness` is treated as the obvious regress-stopper while relational time is uniquely unstable.

### 5.4 Relational dynamic alternatives exist

Recent and contemporary literature also includes relational or B-theoretic attempts to preserve becoming/passage-like structure without a metaphysically privileged NOW. These proposals are controversial, but they further show that:

\[
NoAbsoluteNOW
\not\Rightarrow
NoTemporalStructureBeyondStaticLabeling.
\]

### 5.5 Verdict on Test D

\[
\boxed{TestD=FAIL.}
\]

No neutral-incoherence theorem was obtained.

## 7. Interpretation Capture — strengthened formulation

The earlier audit introduced **Interpretation Capture**. The present four-way audit strengthens it.

### Interpretation Capture Principle (ICP)

Let `D` be a purported independent datum and `M` a contested metaphysical interpretation.

If:

1. `D` can be exhaustively specified in vocabulary shared by both theories;
2. the target theory differs only by adding `M(D)`;
3. no phenomenal, semantic, explanatory, or coherence test independently selects `M`;

then:

\[
\boxed{
D\text{ does not independently support }M.
}
\]

Applied here:

\[
D=\text{ordinary temporal/present experience},
\]

\[
M(D)=PresenceSimpliciter(D).
\]

All four discrimination tests fail. So the `PresenceSimpliciter` witness is interpretation-captured.

## 8. Relation to Conitzer and Hare

Conitzer's result remains important and should **not** be downgraded incorrectly.

His conditional pressure is:

\[
A\text{-theoretic distinguished NOW}
\Rightarrow
pressure\ toward\ distinguished\ I.
\]

In the presence-of-experience form:

\[
PresenceSimpliciter(E)
\Rightarrow
Distinguished(I,NOW)\text{ pair/stage pressure}.
\]

What fails is the upstream inference from shared experience to `PresenceSimpliciter`.

Hare also remains an important positive neighbor because perspectival realism shows that subject-perspectival metaphysics can be taken seriously. But Hare's own comparison of tense realism and perspectival realism is evidence that temporal privilege and perspectival privilege can come apart rather than automatically forcing one another from ordinary experience.

## 10. Reopen conditions

Do not reopen the Presence route merely because a new author says that the present feels vivid, live, immediate, flowing, or available.

Reopen only if a source supplies at least one of:

1. **Phenomenal discriminator**: a clearly specified experiential feature that relational models cannot instantiate.
2. **Semantic-to-ontology theorem**: a non-question-begging argument from irreducible temporal content to irreducible worldly presentness.
3. **Explanatory impossibility result**: proof that every relational explanation fails, rather than merely incurs costs.
4. **Neutral incoherence theorem**: a contradiction/regress specific to relational presence and avoided by simpliciter presence.
5. **Privilege-sensitive evidence**: empirical or epistemic data whose likelihood differs between relational and simpliciter models.

Absent one of these, further ordinary `presence` literature is low-value for the absolute axis.
