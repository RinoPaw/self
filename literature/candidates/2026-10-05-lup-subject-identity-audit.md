# LUP Subject-Identity Audit — Unity, Split Brains, Overlap, and Non-Discrete Subjects

> Date: 2026-10-05
>
> Status: candidate literature audit. Do not treat as authority without later synthesis.
>
> Target question:
>
> \[
> \boxed{Does\ numerical\ subject\ identity\ constitutively\ require\ one\ phenomenal\ field?}
> \]

## 1. Executive result

The literature does **not** currently support the unconditional metaphysical principle

\[
SameSubject(e_i,e_j,t)\Rightarrow PhenomenallyUnified(e_i,e_j,t)
\]

as a theorem or analytic truth.

The strongest defensible result is conditional:

\[
\boxed{
PhenomenalField\text{-}Individuation
+ ConsciousnessEssentialSubjecthood
\Rightarrow
DisjointFields\to DistinctSubjects
}
\]

This is materially useful for the Locality from Unity Principle (LUP), but it means LUP inherits a subject-ontology premise. Universal-I can resist by adopting a substantive/animalist/primitive bearer whose numerical identity outruns phenomenal unity.

At the same time, several lines make that escape non-free:

- Schechter argues that split-brain phenomenal disunity supports two subjects of experience even while preserving one person;
- Mørch argues that phenomenal overlap between conscious subjects is contradictory on a deflationary bundle view and highly unparsimonious on a substantive-subject view;
- recent subject-counting work treats subjects as maximal phenomenal-unity bundles and develops intermediate / indeterminate subject counts under partial or intransitive unity;
- Osborn argues that composite subjects are coherent on a thin subject conception, so anti-combination / anti-universal conclusions cannot rest only on the Jamesian intuition.

The live fork is therefore not simply `Unity Thesis true/false`. It is:

\[
\boxed{
SubjectIdentityGroundedInPhenomenalOrganization
\quad|\quad
PrimitiveOrExtraPhenomenalSubjectIdentity
}
\]

## 2. Bayne: important, but weaker than the previous LUP use

### Source

Tim Bayne, *The Unity of Consciousness* (OUP, 2010).

Reading level: publisher chapter abstracts + current SEP treatment; not full book reread in this pass.

### What is supported

Bayne's Unity Thesis says, roughly, that a conscious creature's synchronous conscious states are phenomenally unified. His project gives a substantive and influential model of a total phenomenal field.

However, the current SEP treatment explicitly characterizes Bayne's necessity claim as **biological necessity**, understood via organismic design specifications. This matters for the project: Bayne does not by himself establish that numerical subject identity metaphysically or constitutively requires phenomenal unity.

So the previous inference

\[
BayneUT\Rightarrow MetaphysicalIdentityCriterion
\]

is too strong.

Bayne remains evidence for a unity-bounded conception of ordinary conscious organization, not a standalone metaphysical theorem about all possible subjects.

## 3. Schechter: strongest direct split-brain support for field-bounded subjects

### Source

Elizabeth Schechter, *Self-Consciousness and "Split" Brains: The Minds' I* (OUP, 2018), especially ch. 2 and ch. 4.

Reading level: publisher chapter abstracts + current SEP reconstruction.

### Result

Schechter explicitly argues for two subjects of experience in split-brain cases. The central structure is:

- both hemispheres support conscious experiential elements;
- unity obtains within each hemisphere system but not across them;
- therefore there are two subjective perspectives;
- a single subject cannot stand to two independent simultaneous perspectives in the same way an ordinary subject stands to one.

This gives a direct identity-pressure argument:

\[
TwoIndependentPhenomenalPerspectives
\to
TwoSubjectsOfExperience.
\]

Importantly, Schechter separates **subject count** from **person count**: a split-brain human can be one person while hosting two subjects of experience. This helps the project avoid organism-level individuation as a shortcut.

## 4. Split-brain literature blocks an easy theorem

The current SEP review distinguishes the structural question (one stream, two streams, partial streams) from the subject-count question. Even if two streams occur, it does not follow automatically that there is one or two subjects; subject individuation remains a substantive issue.

The literature contains live interpretations on which:

- one organism has one switching unified stream;
- one organism hosts two subjects / streams;
- consciousness is partially unified;
- the very question of a determinate subject count can become unstable.

So empirical disunity is evidence relevant to subject individuation, but it does not independently settle the metaphysics of subject identity.

## 5. Mørch: a stronger anti-overlap route

### Source

Hedda Hassel Mørch, “Why Consciousness Can't Overlap” (manuscript; latest public metadata/abstract located through PhilPapers/PhilArchive/author profile).

Reading level: abstract/readable metadata only in this pass. Do not attribute detailed internal steps beyond the public abstract.

### Publicly stated result

Mørch argues that overlapping consciousness — a unified conscious system having parts that are themselves unified conscious systems — should be rejected.

Her stated fork is:

- on a deflationary bundle view of subjects, overlap yields a contradiction;
- on a substantive view of subjects, overlap remains possible but is highly unparsimonious.

This is more useful to LUP than Bayne if the needed premise is phenomenal-field individuation, because it attacks shared-token / nested-field structures directly.

But it still does not establish the unconditional principle that one primitive bearer cannot own several completely disconnected fields. The substantive-subject horn remains conceptually available.

## 6. Roelofs: between-subject unity prevents local-boundary dogmatism

### Source

Luke Roelofs, “The Unity of Consciousness, Within Subjects and Between Subjects,” *Philosophical Studies* 173 (2016), 3199–3221.

Reading level: metadata/abstract plus existing project note; not full-text reread in this pass.

Roelofs argues that phenomenal unity between subjects is conceptually live and develops experience-sharing style structures. This blocks:

\[
DifferentOrganisms\Rightarrow NecessarilyDisunified.
\]

Therefore locality cannot be derived from organism boundaries alone. The project needs an individuation principle at the level of phenomenal fields / perspectives, not anatomy.

## 7. Schwitzgebel–Nelson: subject count may be non-discrete

### Source

Eric Schwitzgebel & Sophie R. Nelson, “When Counting Conscious Subjects, the Result Needn’t Always Be a Determinate Whole Number,” *Philosophical Psychology* 39(3) (2026), 847–867; published online 16 June 2025.

Reading level: author-hosted HTML/PDF and publisher/search full-text excerpts read in this pass.

### Key move

They explicitly work with a thin conception on which conscious subjects are bundles of experiences individuated by maximal phenomenal-unity relations.

Under standard transitive maximal unity, this yields discrete subjects. But they explore:

- intermediate / indeterminate phenomenal unity;
- intransitive or partial unity;
- overlapping phenomenal fields sharing token experiences;
- cases in which subject number is not a determinate whole number.

This produces a major correction to a binary LUP:

\[
Disunity\not\Rightarrow AlwaysExactlyTwoSubjects.
\]

A better structural claim is:

\[
\boxed{
SubjectStructureTracksMaximalPhenomenalOrganization
}
\]

where that organization may be discrete, overlapping, vague, or intermediate.

For the project, this still hurts a simple Universal-I covering wholly disconnected local fields: if subject individuation is phenomenal-organization-first, universal subjecthood requires actual connective structure. But the result is a **partition/overlap topology**, not necessarily a clean integer partition.

## 8. Osborn: composite subjects remain a live counterpressure

### Source

Nicholas Osborn, “Subjects Simply Sum into Subjects,” *Dialectica* 78(3) (2024), 87–105.

Reading level: journal/PhilPapers abstract and public summary read in this pass.

Osborn challenges the Jamesian intuition that subjects cannot simply sum into larger subjects. He proposes a thin conception of subjects on which subject summation is positively conceivable.

This is important because LUP must not rely on the intuition:

\[
ManySubjects\Rightarrow NoGreaterSubject.
\]

Even if local subjects exist, a higher-order subject may still be coherent. What matters for Universal-I is whether that higher subject possesses a phenomenally integrated field spanning the locals, not whether composition is intuitively odd.

## 9. Animalism / substantive bearer horn

A recurring countermodel is that subject identity is fixed by organism, causal system, primitive bearer, or other extra-phenomenal facts. On such a view one subject could in principle host two phenomenally disconnected streams.

This is the cleanest counterexample schema to constitutive LUP:

\[
SameBearer(S)+TwoDisconnectedFields(F_1,F_2)
\not\Rightarrow Contradiction.
\]

Therefore any strong LUP must argue for consciousness-essential / phenomenally individuated subjects rather than simply assume them.

## 10. Updated LUP candidate

Replace the unconditional identity thesis:

\[
SameSubject\Rightarrow OnePhenomenalField
\]

with a conditional structural principle:

### PFIP — Phenomenal-Field Individuation Principle

\[
\boxed{
If\ subject\ identity\ is\ constituted\ by\ maximal\ phenomenal\ organization,
\ then\ numerically\ one\ subject\ cannot\ span\ wholly\ disconnected\ maximal\ fields.
}
\]

More schematically:

\[
PhenomenalIndividuation(S)
+ MaximalDisconnection(F_1,F_2)
\Rightarrow
S(F_1)\neq S(F_2).
\]

This is weaker than old LUP but better supported.

## 11. Consequence for Universal-I

A substantive Universal-I now faces a sharper fork:

### U1 — Phenomenally constituted Universal-I

It must show a genuine unity / connectivity structure spanning all local experiences.

If local fields are maximally disconnected, PFIP blocks one subject.

### U2 — Primitive / extra-phenomenal Universal-I

It can own disconnected fields by brute bearer identity, but then it owes an independent account of:

- what fixes that bearer identity;
- why wholly disconnected experiences belong to that one bearer;
- what explanatory work “one subject” performs beyond a global ownership label.

Thus Universal-I remains live, but the burden becomes explicit.

## 12. Current verdict

\[
\boxed{
LUP\ is\ not\ an\ unconditional\ metaphysical\ theorem.
}
\]

The strongest current route is:

\[
\boxed{
PhenomenalFieldIndividuation
+ MaximalDisconnection
\Rightarrow
NoSinglePhenomenallyConstitutedUniversalSubject.
}
\]

This is a real locality gain, but it relocates the deepest burden to the metaphysics of subjects:

\[
\boxed{
What\ constitutes\ numerical\ subject\ identity?
}
\]

That should be the next direct audit before updating `synthesis/current-position.md`.
