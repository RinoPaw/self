# C1: Neutral Contrast Class and Hyperintensional Grounding

> 2026-10-08；research criteria, conditional finite examples; **not a non-reduction theorem**.  
> Extends: [Mode Amalgamation and Neutral Reduct](mode-amalgamation-neutral-reduct.md).

## Neutral theories must be compared fairly

Let B(R) be a specified neutral base for a candidate totality R, and FP(R) its alleged fundamental first-person mode facts.

- **B0:** only shared objective history. Weak: omits rich local phenomenality.
- **B1:** B0 plus real subjects, local phenomenal profiles, psychophysical/functional and relational facts, described without treating first-person *obtaining modes* as primitives. This is C0's serious competitor base.
- **B2:** B1 plus a neutral tuple encoding *all* mode assignments. A complete encoding is trivially possible for finite typed data but may smuggle the explanandum into B2.

Conclusions from a B0 counterexample do not automatically defeat B1. Conversely, finding a B2 encoding does not establish grounding reduction.

## Four questions to keep separate

**Expression/encoding:** Can finite typed first-person data be written as neutral tuples? Yes for the formal representation; this concerns a language and decoder.

**Relative determination:** In an explicitly chosen model class M, is it true that whenever B(R)=B(R'), then FP(R)=FP(R')?

\[
Determination_M(B,FP)
\iff
\forall R,R'\in M\,[B(R)=B(R')\to FP(R)=FP(R')].
\]

**Metaphysical supervenience:** Does analogous determination hold among genuinely metaphysically possible total realities? A finite toy class cannot settle its modal range.

**Grounding/identity reduction:** Even if supervenience holds, is the first-person fact *in virtue of* a neutral fact, or identical to it? This is a hyperintensional explanatory claim. It is not entailed merely by mutual encodability or model determination.

## Conditional finite contrast

Fix B1-like entries for one objective history, two subjects and their local phenomenal states. Construct R1 and R2 with identical neutral facts but different primitive mode flags. This proves *only* that the chosen signature without further bridge axioms does not force mode facts. It does not prove both worlds metaphysically admissible; C1 may require all conscious subjects to instantiate a mode, and C0 may supply a further constitution law. A mere permutation of mode names is also not a new non-isomorphic world and cannot serve as evidence of primitive privilege.

For a non-circular C1 case we would need: (i) a defensible admissible modal class, (ii) a B1 neutral base rich enough to capture real local phenomenality, (iii) non-isomorphic variation or an independent hyperintensional grounding argument, and (iv) a law of cross-mode/global coherence. Naming the modes “constitutive” only announces a primitive hypothesis.

Likewise, a C0 victory would require a B1-based grounding account that actually explains first-person subjectivity, not just a lossless bookkeeping translation.

## Source and status

The SEP *Supervenience* §3.5 explicitly distinguishes supervenience, grounding and ontological dependence: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/supervenience/ .

Lipman's *Standpoints: Time and Subjectivity* (2026), chapter 3 publisher abstract, emphasizes fact identity as a prior issue to grounding; consulted as a **chapter abstract**, not new full-text verification: https://doi.org/10.1093/9780198921318.003.0005 .

**Verdict:** Neither a relative twin model against B0/B1 nor a neutral tuple encoding proves C1's irreducibility or C0's reduction. The unfilled target is metaphysical identity/grounding relative to a genuinely competitive B1.
