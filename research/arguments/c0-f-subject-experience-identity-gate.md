# C0-F Subject–Experience Identity Gate — Does Felt Ownership Need an Additional Mode?

> 2026-10-08 · **research result: a serious positive constitution candidate for C0-F, with named gaps**.  
> This is an **adversarial reconstruction**, not a proof that C0 is the actual ontology.  
> Builds on [three C0 grounding packages](../models/c0-phenomenal-constitution-packages.md), [B1 stress test](b1-grounding-adversarial-test.md), [B1 residual](b1-explanatory-residue-audit.md), and [MI / overlap](mi-aim-incidence-audit.md).

## 0. What exactly must C0-F establish?

The strongest question is not whether everyone has their own pain: C0-F already admits all genuine local phenomenal lives. The real question is **what makes a token pain be an experience of a specific subject**.

We must separate four theses:

- **E0 — Actual qualitative occurrence**: a genuine phenomenal quality is instantiated; nobody is merely reported to be conscious.
- **E1 — Constitutive bearer**: who undergoes this token is part of its identity, rather than an incidental observer, causal producer, or reporter.
- **E2 — First-person givenness**: experiencing this quality is *lived* or given from the participant's perspective, without needing a further introspective act.
- **E3 — Irreducible obtaining mode**: in addition to E0–E2 there exists a fundamental fact about the *manner in which* the experience obtains, not identically determined or grounded by the bearer-indexed phenomenal token.
- **E4 — One absolute global center**: a unique simpliciter I–NOW realizes all actuality.

E0–E2 are the high-stakes commitments for **real local consciousness**. E3 is where C1 explicitly asks for more fundamental ontology. E4 is C2's further singularity. **E2 does not already mean E3/E4**.

## 1. Strong positive C0-F: property-exemplification events

Henry Taylor, “The relation between subjects and their conscious experiences”, *Philosophical Studies* 177 (2020), 3493–3507 (online 2019), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-019-01379-w, provides a detailed **ontology of ownership**, not just a physical-state catalog.

Adapting the property-exemplification (PE) model associated with Jaegwon Kim:

\[
e=\langle s,q,t\rangle, \qquad
Exists(e)\iff Exemplifies(s,q,t).
\]

Here s is a genuine conscious subject, q a genuine phenomenal property *of the subject*, and t the relevant time. The experience e is the event constituted by the subject's instantiation of that property. The thesis is ontological: **the phenomenal property modifies the subject**; it is not an independent quality sitting in a separate object that an observer later notices.

Taylor supplies two desiderata:

1. **Token subject necessity**: the very same token experience could not have belonged to some numerically different subject, given PE identity conditions.
2. **Subject event contingency**: the subject could have existed without that very token experience.

PE individuation makes both intelligible:

\[
\langle s,q,t\rangle = \langle s',q',t'\rangle
\iff s=s'\land q=q'\land t=t'.
\]

For instance \(\langle a,\mathrm{pain},t\rangle\) and \(\langle b,\mathrm{pain},t\rangle\) are numerically different, although qualitatively similar. Replacing a by b changes the token event. The subject can still instantiate some other property at another time without destroying their identity.

**What PE genuinely explains**: the *essential bearer/tight connection* between a token phenomenal event and its actual subject. This is a substantive ontological hypothesis about event identity, not an empty one-word reference to ownership.

**What PE does not explain**: why q is genuinely phenomenal, which qualities there are, why they interact with neural mechanisms, why particular instantiation occurs, or how any unique global privileged standpoint is chosen. If q is fundamental, PE has an honest primitive phenomenal stopping point.

### Local uniqueness is not global uniqueness

The PE identity thesis for each token e constrains \(\operatorname{Bearer}(e)\). It neither claims all experiences share a bearer nor provides a fundamental world-selector:

\[
\forall e\;\exists!s\,Bearer(e,s)
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\exists!s\;\forall e\,Bearer(e,s).
\]

Two experiences with different subjects satisfy the left formula and refute the right. Even if **every actual** phenomenal event is owned in this essential way, the C2 global privileged I–NOW is not obtained.

## 2. A critical equivocation: four senses of "mine"

**Marie Guillot**, “I Me Mine: on a Confusion Concerning the Subjective Character of Experience”, *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 8 (2017), 23–53, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s13164-016-0313-4, makes three non-equivalent distinctions:

| Character of experience | What it contributes | What it does NOT establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Qualitative character** | What the pain/redness feels like | Subject-identity/global privilege by itself |
| **For-me-ness** | Its givenness/awareness *to a subject* | Automatic self-awareness or explicit ownership |
| **Me-ness** | Experiential awareness of oneself as the one undergoing it | Automatically equivalent to for-me-ness |
| **Mineness** | Sense that this experience is *my own* | Automatically equivalent to for-me-ness or unique world-selector |

Guillot's formal conclusion is especially helpful: **for-me-ness does not conceptually entail me-ness or mineness**. Her clinical examples are philosophical interpretations and not clinical proof that the properties dissociate in every case. The paper does not endorse C0, C1 or C2 as defined in this project.

PE event-subject dependence is an **ontological bearer claim**; for-me-ness is an **experiential givenness claim**; me-ness and mineness may involve yet further awareness. Equating them is an invalid step. Conversely, letting "a subject has q" mean only "someone's brain registers q" silently deletes E0/E2 and no longer represents the strongest C0-F.

## 3. The phenomenological objection at full strength

The SEP [Phenomenological Approaches to Self-Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/) describes prereflective first-personal givenness as an **intrinsic feature of the experience itself**, not an additional higher-order monitoring act or another observer. That can press C0-F: bare causal ownership would be far too weak.

But it does not automatically vindicate C1/C2. A **thick C0-F** can grant:

\[
Exemplifies(s,q_{\mathrm{genuine\ lived}},t).
\]

The property q is genuinely phenomenal and the event is intrinsically lived by s. This accommodates the datum as **primitive qualitative character of this actual subject's event**, not as a brain report. The costs remain clear:

- primitive q has no deeper phenomenal derivation;
- a constitutive *phenomenal subject–property instantiation* has been assumed;
- whether this thick q is a noncircular, metaphysically legitimate world-side fact is disputed.

A C1 defender needs a **further premise**: any genuinely lived q has a fact-identity/obtaining manner M that **cannot be identical with or grounded by** this thick instantiation fact. A C2 defender needs *another* premise that M is globally singleton. Neither follows merely from acknowledging prereflective self-givenness.

### Serious counterpressure from 2026 literature

Roberto Sá Pereira, “For-me-ness: a critical assessment of the phenomenal mineness thesis”, *Cognitive Processing* (published 4 August 2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10339-026-01383-z, argues that empirical/clinical cases often cited for **universal primitive experiential mineness** fail to discriminate that thesis from representational, egocentric or self-modeling explanations. This is a substantive philosophical challenge, **not a scientific demonstration that prereflective phenomenality does not exist**. The phenomenological and representationalist readings remain contested.

In particular, a successful representational explanation of ownership reports would not, by itself, explain the **actual felt quality q**. E0/E2 and the thesis of *necessarily primitive mineness* must remain separate.

## 4. Phenomenal token sharing: Taylor versus overlap

Taylor explicitly treats bearer identity as part of token-event identity. That conflicts with some interpretations of **Luke Roelofs** (2016), “The Unity of Consciousness, Within Subjects and Between Subjects”, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-016-0658-7, in which a token experience may be *shared* by distinct subjects.

Consider one phenomenal token x and subjects a,b.

- **Strict single-bearer PE**: if x is identified as \(\langle a,q,t\rangle\), the distinct \(\langle b,q,t\rangle\) is a second token (even if phenomenally indistinguishable). The strong sharing claim fails by the chosen PE identity axiom.
- **Generalized bearer incidence**: perhaps x has a constitutive bearer *set* \(B(x)=\{a,b\}\). This can preserve overlap while changing Taylor's identity conditions. But **is it really the same token**, and does genuine phenomenal co-consciousness imply shared ownership? Those are outstanding issues.
- **Two overlapping but distinct events**: a and b have their own PE tokens with shared substructure. This preserves PE and some intersubjectal unity, but it may not meet Roelofs's stronger token-sharing thesis.

The correct conclusion is **not** that overlap is impossible. The strict Taylor package carries a *single-bearer individuation cost*. Even if sharing turns out to be metaphysically possible, a revised plural-bearer world ontology need not include one global absolute selector. C0 as a family is not refuted by rejection of one event identity theory.

## 5. Strongest two-way diagnostic — conservative description versus fact identity

Let \(\mathcal B_{PE}\) describe all actual subject-property-time instantiations and all genuine qualitative q, including prereflective local givenness; a C1 completion proposes further modes \(M\).

**C1 weak completion**: every instance \((s,q,t)\) is assigned a mode label \(m_{s,t}\) exactly determined by the PE token. If M adds no new grounding/fact-identity conditions, then it may be a definitional *coding* of the same local reality. Such a code does not by itself establish new ontic facts.

**C1 strong completion**: two proposed actual realities have **the very same complete PE facts** but differ in some obtaining-mode facts, or a mode is demonstrably constitutive of a token's identity while no legitimate PE base grounds it. This would be a substantive candidate *new fact*, provided both completions are **metaphysically admissible** and their difference survives event/subject relabeling.

The finite representation of this contrast is easy. The serious unresolved work is to justify its metaphysical possibility and **non-reduction**, not to programmatically generate mode flags.

**C2 still faces two gates:** existence of fundamental obtaining modes and their exactly-one cardinality plus centered reflection/localization. Showing local givenness or local single-bearer events satisfies neither additional requirement.

## 7. This round's defensible conclusion

**Positive progress:** C0-F has a **published, serious, explicit candidate** for its most vulnerable issue, the subject–experience intimacy relation: bearer-indexed phenomenal property exemplification. The challenge is no longer "C0 says consciousness belongs to somebody, full stop."

**Important limitation:** This does not finish consciousness metaphysics. Taylor primarily explains **token ownership and event identity**, taking genuine phenomenal properties for granted. Guillot blocks several easy slides from for-me-ness to mineness and selfhood. Sá Pereira offers a 2026 criticism of the necessity of a primitive mineness layer. Roelofs exposes a live competing understanding of phenomenal token sharing.

**C1's clearest remaining attack:** show that a thick, genuinely felt PE event \(\langle s,q,t\rangle\) is metaphysically insufficient **because a distinct obtaining-mode fact is constitutive**; provide independent event/fact identity and grounding reasons rather than merely asserting that "experience is first-personal."

**C2's extra attack:** even after establishing that new mode, give independent reasons why one actual reality requires exactly one global privileged center.

The additional research value now lies in **the contested ontic identity of genuinely lived q**, and in whether shareable experiential tokens defeat the single-bearer event identity theory. Repeating "C0 lacks the feeling of being me" without disentangling these questions will not improve the argument.
