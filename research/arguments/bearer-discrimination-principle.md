# Bearer Discrimination Principle — Subject Identity After PFIP

> Date: 2026-10-05
>
> Status: argument-level reconstruction after comparative subject-ontology audit.
>
> Goal: identify the burden inherited by any theory that assigns one numerical subject across phenomenally disconnected fields.

## 1. Starting point

PFIP established only a conditional locality result:

\[
PhenomenalIndividuation + MaximalDisconnection
\Rightarrow
DistinctSubjects.
\]

A Universal-I can escape by claiming that subject identity is fixed extra-phenomenally.

The comparative ontology audit shows that this escape divides into two importantly different forms:

1. a causal / organismic / functional bearer whose identity is independently specified;
2. a primitive bearer whose ownership relation is fundamental.

These should not be collapsed into one horn.

## 2. BDP — Bearer Discrimination Principle

Suppose fields \(F_1,\ldots,F_n\) are phenomenally disconnected at the relevant level.

A theory claims:

\[
\exists S\;\forall i\;OwnedBy(F_i,S).
\]

Then it owes a fact \(D\) such that:

\[
D\Rightarrow OneSubjectAssignment(F_1,\ldots,F_n)
\]

and \(D\) is not merely a restatement of the one-subject assignment itself.

So:

\[
\boxed{
DisconnectedFields + OneSubjectClaim
\Rightarrow
IndependentBearerDiscriminator.
}
\]

Call this the **Bearer Discrimination Principle (BDP)**.

## 3. Why BDP is needed

Without BDP, three assignments can be structurally compatible with the same phenomenal base:

\[
A_1: \text{one bearer owns all fields},
\]

\[
A_2: \text{one bearer per field},
\]

\[
A_3: \text{some intermediate grouping}.
\]

If the theory provides no fact other than `OwnedBy` itself to distinguish them, the ownership assignment performs no explanatory work. It simply labels the preferred partition.

This is structurally analogous to the project's earlier factorization / selection lessons:

\[
Selection\neq Privileging
\]

and

\[
NamingAnAssignment\neq ExplainingTheAssignment.
\]

BDP therefore asks for a genuine individuation basis rather than a preferred notation.

## 4. How the three ontologies fare

### 4.1 Phenomenal-field ontology

Discriminator:

\[
D_{PH}=MaximalPhenomenalOrganization.
\]

This gives strong explanatory discrimination. But if fields are maximally disconnected, it yields plurality rather than Universal-I.

Thus:

\[
BDP + PH \Rightarrow \neg UniversalI
\]

conditional on actual global phenomenal disconnection.

### 4.2 Causal / organismic bearer ontology

Potential discriminator:

\[
D_C=OneCausallyIntegratedBearer.
\]

This can genuinely distinguish assignments if there is an independently individuated causal/functional system whose unity is not defined by the desired subject assignment.

But two further burdens remain:

1. **system individuation** — why this causal whole rather than its parts or a larger enclosing system?
2. **subject relevance** — why does causal-system identity determine experiential ownership?

So:

\[
CausalUnity\not\Rightarrow SubjectUnity
\]

without an additional psychophysical bridge.

For ordinary organisms this bridge may be plausible; for a Universal-I spanning all organisms it becomes much harder. Mere causal connectedness of the universe is too cheap because it also relates systems we ordinarily treat as distinct subjects.

### 4.3 Primitive bearer ontology

Potential discriminator:

\[
D_P=PrimitiveSubjectFact.
\]

This technically satisfies BDP only if the primitive fact has determinate content that distinguishes one universal bearer from many primitive bearers.

But if the explanation is simply:

\[
OneSubject\ because\ OneSubject,
\]

BDP is not substantively met.

A serious primitive theory therefore needs either:

- a primitive individuation law;
- a primitive ownership topology;
- a global necessity principle;
- or some independently motivated constraint on permissible bearer assignments.

Otherwise primitive Universal-I remains coherent but explanatorily idle.

## 5. Universal-I trilemma

Universal-I now faces:

\[
\boxed{
UniversalI\Rightarrow
\begin{cases}
GlobalPhenomenalConnectivity,\\
IndependentGlobalBearer + PsychophysicalOwnershipBridge,\\
PrimitiveBearer + IndependentAssignmentPrinciple.
\end{cases}}
\]

The first horn is directly testable against phenomenal-field structure.

The second horn requires a real globally individuated bearer and a reason that bearer identity determines one subject.

The third horn is metaphysically possible but risks brute reassignment unless the primitive assignment principle has independent motivation.

## 6. Relation to open individualism

Open-individualist / one-self views are best classified by which BDP horn they occupy.

If the claim is that all first-person immediacy is literally one phenomenal subject, it owes global phenomenal connectivity or a reason why connectivity is unnecessary.

If the claim is that one self underlies all local minds despite disconnected phenomenology, it is a bearer theory and must specify the bearer discriminator.

If numerical identity of the one self is simply primitive, the view moves to the primitive horn and inherits the assignment-principle burden.

Therefore `all experiences are mine` is not yet a subject-identity theory until its discriminator is explicit.

## 7. What BDP does not prove

BDP does not establish that only phenomenal individuation is legitimate.

It does not refute animalism, causal bearer theories, substance dualism, or primitive subjects.

It also does not prove that the phenomenal fields of ordinary subjects are globally maximally disconnected.

Its role is narrower:

\[
\boxed{
OneSubjectAcrossDisconnection\ cannot\ be\ explanatory\ merely\ by\ asserting\ one\ ownership\ label.
}
\]

## 8. Updated locality chain

The current locality route is better represented as:

### Route PH

\[
MaximalDisconnection
+ PhenomenalIndividuation
\Rightarrow
NoUniversalSubject.
\]

### Route C

\[
MaximalDisconnection
+ CausalBearerTheory
\Rightarrow
Need(GlobalBearer + OwnershipBridge).
\]

### Route P

\[
MaximalDisconnection
+ PrimitiveSubjectTheory
\Rightarrow
Need(PrimitiveAssignmentPrinciple).
\]

Thus the locality question no longer depends on one controversial universal Unity Thesis. It becomes a discriminator test across rival subject ontologies.

## 9. Current verdict

\[
\boxed{
PFIP\ blocks\ phenomenally\ constituted\ UniversalI;
BDP\ pressures\ extra\text{-}phenomenal\ UniversalI.
}
\]

This still falls short of a theorem for Selective Opening, but it closes an important escape: moving from a failed global phenomenal field to “one bearer anyway” now creates a specific explanatory debt rather than preserving Universal-I for free.
