# C0-F Thick Phenomenal Property Dilemma — Experiential Nature and Ontic Modes

## 1. Precise explanandum: one actual genuine felt experience

Fix a **genuine phenomenal truth**, not an objective report:

- s is an actual conscious subject at t;
- q is a specifically *experiential* property such that for s to instantiate q at t is genuinely to undergo a determinate felt character;
- e is the token event of that instantiation.

PE candidate: the existence and identity of e are fixed by the event constituted by s exemplifying q at t. In the relevant interpretation, q is a property **of the experiencing subject**, not an experience-object requiring a second perceiver.

This *already accepts* real felt qualia. It does **not** presume either physical reduction or unique global I–NOW, and it does not magically explain the inner nature of q.

The critical question: does this thick event leave an ontically separate obtaining-mode fact M(s,t) unaccounted for?

## 2. The q trilemma: thin, thick, or mode-inflated

The single letter q has obscured three **noninterchangeable** ontological packages.

| q interpretation | What is instantiated? | Result for C0-F |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T0: Thin** | neural, dispositional, behavioral, third-person-accessible, or purely representation-bearing feature with no claim to felt occurrence | The event alone does **not** establish genuine phenomenality. If C0-F used only T0, the attack succeeds against this weakened package. |
| **T1: Thick-experiential** | subject's genuinely lived phenomenal quality as a fundamental world-side property; any local prereflective givenness accepted is intrinsic to this experiential instantiation | E0 felt occurrence and local subjective character are granted, and Taylor's PE event supplies bearer identity. The *why this q exists* grounding question stops at a primitive; a separate M is **not entailed** merely by T1's explanandum. |
| **T2: Mode-loaded** | q is defined so that genuinely felt means **the fact additionally obtains in irreducible perspective mode M**, with no grounding in a subject-bearing qualitative event | This may be a coherent **C1-style posit**, but it cannot be introduced into T1's adequacy test without an independent argument. C2 additionally needs uniqueness and localization. |

**Dilemma for the C1 critic:** If its objection says only “T0 omits real feeling,” thick C0-F accepts the objection by moving to T1. If it says “T1 still lacks M,” the critic must motivate the stronger T2 **without relying on T2 to define real phenomenality**. Naming M as “what feeling really is” states the disagreement; it does not settle it.

**Burden for C0-F:** T1 has *fundamental phenomenal primitives*. It explains the event's essential bearer structure but does not reductively explain why this phenomenal quality obtains rather than some other quality, why subject boundaries have their actual shape, or how physical interaction works. Treating T1 as an adequate ontic root is an explicit and contestable metaphysical stopping rule, not a solution to the hard problem.

## 3. Nida-Rümelin's positive correction: the property has the subject as its bearer

Martine Nida-Rümelin, “The experience property frame work: a misleading paradigm”, *Synthese* 195 (2018), 3361–3387 (published online 2016), argues against **qualia as properties of experience-objects** and in favor of **experiential properties instantiated by experiencing subjects**.

On this view:

- undergoing the experience of blue can be framed as **a subject's exemplifying being phenomenally presented with blue**;
- it is a mistake to require that the subject observe another item, an experience with a quale, in order to become conscious;
- the framework distinction is philosophically consequential, not merely new notation.

This connects closely to Taylor's PE event model (Taylor himself cites Nida-Rümelin). It blocks a particular **regress objection**: if every instance of awareness demanded another conscious act directed at a free-standing phenomenal object, that picture presupposes the *experience-as-object* framework the account rejects.

**Limit:** A subject's being the bearer of a property does not prove that the property is phenomenally felt. Nida-Rümelin's *experiential property* qualification already includes that demanding condition. The positive account therefore supplies a **non-objectifying structure and a place for primitive felt quality**, while q's fundamental nature remains the unresolved explanandum.

## 4. Deflationary vs inflationary constitutivity is a real fork, not a verbal victory

The SEP [Phenomenological Approaches to Self-Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/) holds up an influential view on which **prereflective givenness is intrinsic to the experience** and is *not an extra act/object* added over and above lived experience. That view is compatible with there being first-person phenomenal facts, but does not settle **hyperintensional ontic fact identity** or global singleton.

Raphaël Millière, “Constitutive Self-Consciousness”, *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 103(3) (2025), 617–637, distinguishes:

- **deflationary** constitutional claim: a minimal consciousness-related awareness follows from elucidating consciousness at a basic level;
- **inflationary** claim: every experience must contain a further determinate feature of phenomenal self-awareness not contained in the modest definition. His *abstract* argues the inflationary thesis is insufficiently supported.

This maps usefully—but only **analogically**, not as Millière's own C0/C1 taxonomy—to our problem:

1. **Constitutive-within-event**: for-someone givenness is an integral feature of q or the subject's lived instantiation; T1 may already include it. This supports local phenomenality without independently positing M.
2. **Additional-ontic-mode**: first-person obtaining is a separate fundamental fact M not identical with or grounded by thick T1. That is the substantive C1 claim; its evidence must survive the former explanation.
3. **Global absolute singleton**: even successful independent M would leave C1 as a competitor to C2.

**Important non-equivalence**: phenomenology's word *mode of givenness* is not automatically our formal ontic *mode of obtaining*. Both can be “irreducible” in a phenomenological or conceptual sense without their being distinct world-side obtaining facts.

## 5. O'Conaill: experiencing does not transparently reveal all of subjecthood

Donnchadh O'Conaill, “Subjecthood transparency”, *Analysis* 84(3) (2024), 504–511, critiques Philip Goff's route from transparent phenomenal concepts to a transparent complete essence of **being a subject**.

His specific pressure point:

- a subject may possibly persist during **dreamless sleep or coma** without occurrent experience (he does not claim this metaphysical possibility is established);
- knowing phenomenal qualities in full does **not alone determine** whether subjecthood requires continually having some conscious episode;
- the metaphysical **capacity to experience** and its **present exercise** may differ, and the full grounds of the capacity cannot be read automatically from a phenomenal concept.

Consequences for the present inquiry:

- Taylor's occurrent e cannot serve as the complete identity criterion for s unless a separate theory of subjecthood justifies this;
- equally, a C1 critic cannot claim that introspective grasp of *what it's like* automatically reveals the essence, individuation, or non-reducible existence of a mode M;
- uncertainty about subject persistence must **not** be mistaken for independent evidence of a globally privileged continuous I–NOW.

This is **an underdetermination objection** to conceptual transparency, not positive proof of C0 neutral grounding.

## 6. Two distinct and noncircular research paths to a C1 winner

### Path A: admissible-modal contrast

Construct two genuinely **metaphysically admissible** total realities R and R' which agree on the *complete* T1 subject-bearing experiential facts—including q's lived quality, bearer, temporal structure, and relational identities—but differ in an obtaining-mode truth not reducible to renaming.

A typed formal structure or two rows of mode flags is too weak. Why do the differing mode facts *actually mean* something distinct while every T1 lived fact is unchanged? What independent individuation principle makes these worlds metaphysically possible?

A successful contrast would supply a plausible **non-supervenience** line against this specified C0-F, not yet the complete hyperintensional grounding story or any singleton proof.

### Path B: essential fact-identity / grounding argument

One can instead argue that T1 cannot ground the *identity of some determinate real phenomenal fact* because a separately understood constitutive manner M is essential to that fact. This path **need not** first produce two possible worlds with different mode assignments: metaphysical supervenience and hyperintensional ground are distinct.

But it must supply:

1. **non-mode circular identification** of the determinate lived fact allegedly omitted;
2. an independently plausible essence/constitution criterion;
3. a reason that thick q + subject-instantiation cannot satisfy it;
4. a positive description of how M **does** satisfy it, beyond relabeling;
5. a comparison with C1's many-M coherent reality before concluding that C2's single M is necessary.

We currently do not have a decisive A or B.

## 7. Small demonstration: bearer necessity and first-person privilege are separate

Given PE event identity e=(s,q,t), two events e_a=(a,pain,t) and e_b=(b,pain,t) may both actually obtain. Each event is token-necessarily tied to its constituting subject under the chosen identity theory. Neither is “less phenomenally real” in T1.

This is not itself a full proof of metaphysical possibility. It is a clear **conditional counterexample to a quantifier inference**:

\[
\forall e\,\exists!s\;Bearer(e,s)
\not\Rightarrow
\exists!s\,\forall e\;Bearer(e,s).
\]

If C1 modes are added, their cardinality remains separate from subject bearer count; if C2 is proposed, absolute priority is *additional* and needs independent grounding/evidence.

## 8. Verdict and next disciplined step

**New positive information:** Nida-Rümelin supplies a genuinely worked out **subject-property** ontology that avoids an extra experience-as-object; Millière sharpens the distinction between modest constitutive consciousness and inflated self-consciousness; O'Conaill breaks an attempted shortcut from experiential concepts to the full essence of a subject.

**Newly precise limit:** The C1 argument “C0-F has not explained why q is felt” has no automatic conclusion. If q is a **primitive genuinely phenomenal property**, lack of further metaphysical explanation is a grounding debt, *not yet proof of a second obtaining fact*. If q was only a nonphenomenal placeholder, the stronger objection succeeds only against thin C0.

**Open question:** is it independently legitimate to treat subject-instantiations of genuinely felt properties as fundamental world-side facts, or must phenomenal nature be constituted by an irreducible mode? The deciding route is **fact-identity/grounding** or **admissible modal contrast** with T1 held fixed.

Further papers or toy models that do not help pass one of these gates should not be presented as progress on C1/C2.
